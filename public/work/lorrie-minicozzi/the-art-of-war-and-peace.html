Artist Statement

In a fractured world, where conflict often overshadows compassion, The Art of War and Peace stands as both a reckoning and a plea. This sculptural work is carved into the interior shell of a globe, a concave terrain that cradles the weight of history and the hope of tomorrow.

Across its surface, the faces of history emerge: warriors and visionaries, tyrants and saints, poets and revolutionaries. Washington and Tubman, Caesar and Gandhi, Hitler and Bertha von Suttner, Jesus and Buddha. Each portrait, meticulously drawn by hand in graphite, represents an individual whose life and legacy contribute to our shared human story. Together, they remind us that history is not written in absolutes, but in the tension between power and mercy, conquest and compassion.

Above this foundation, the sculptural hemisphere rises: a landscape divided between ruin and renewal. On one side, the ravages of war are carved into the Earth itself: bombed-out towns, shattered weapons, barbed wire, and the skeletal remains of industry. The ground is not passive; it is wounded, scorched, and screaming. Throughout this devastated terrain, sculptural interpretations of iconic works by Francisco Goya, John Singer Sargent, Eugène Delacroix, and other masters bring the human experience of conflict to life. Amid the destruction, figures in battered uniforms and shredded clothing rise, worn and weary, yet still lifting infants toward the sky. Their poses echo the drama and movement of the great masters, as they offer the next generation to a world that must do better.

Opposite this terrain, nature reclaims its place. Ferns unfurl, flowers bloom, and the stream that cuts through the landscape glows with an iridescent blue-green shimmer, a vein of healing that divides destruction from rebirth. It is not naïve beauty; it is resilience. It is the promise of peace born from pain.

This work is not neutral. It is a call to unity, a demand for empathy, and a tribute to the children who deserve a future free from hatred and war. The figures do not stand alone; they rise together, across borders, across histories, across wounds.

The Art of War and Peace is a sculptural prayer, one that carries the faces of our past and the fragile hope of our future. May it be heard.
